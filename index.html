05 · BSc thesis · ICAI

Design and simulation of a CFRP monocoque for a Formula Student car.

Altair HyperMesh with the OptiStruct solver. Written in three months, alongside the IFS-08 season, to give the team a complete knowledge base for its first composite chassis.

Grade

10/10

Supervisor: Ángel Rubio López · Read it in the Comillas repository ↗

Problem & objectives

4,000 Nm/deg at about 20 kg.

The IFS-07 ran a steel tubular spaceframe of about 38.5 kg with a torsional stiffness of around 2,300 Nm/deg. For four years, final-degree projects had approached a monocoque for the team. This one set out to close the loop. The objectives:

  • Design a rule-compliant monocoque to the Formula Student rules.
  • Reach 4,000 Nm/deg at roughly 20 kg.
  • Leave the team a working method for composite optimisation.

Design · SolidWorks

A surface built around the rules

Cockpit and internal cross-section templates, the Percy driver template and hoop positions set the minimum geometry. The IFS-07 suspension points were kept and checked in Optimum Kinematics, so existing sizing calculations stayed valid.

Model · HyperMesh

Mesh, materials and load paths

  • Shell mesh with a 5 mm target element size.
  • Suspension links modelled as 1D bars, with loads carried into the tub through RBE2 elements.
  • Sandwich laminate: Gurit carbon plies (IMC unidirectional, RC200T 0/90 and XC411 ±45 cloth) over a Dura-Core II 5052 aluminium honeycomb core.

Process tree

Free-size, size, then shape the plies

For every load step (main hoop, front hoop, TSAC, harness, suspension and impact), free-size optimisation minimises compliance under a volume-fraction constraint. Sizing optimisation then minimises mass with the Hill failure criterion kept below 1. The ply shapes that come out are redrawn into manufacturable, balanced plies before the final analyses.

Optimisation results

Let the loads draw the laminate

Free-size results show where material is needed for each load case. The thickness contours become ply sets, which I redrew into seven ply shapes.

Iteration

Seven laminates to the final one

The first monocoque laminate weighed 56.88 kg at 2,742.66 Nm/deg. I iterated ply order, honeycomb thickness and material per ply. Laminate 007, the final one, lands at 19.78 kg and 4,089 Nm/deg: 31 plies in 7 shapes.

Validation

Torsion, and every rule load case

Rotation along the car rises where the cockpit opening begins, because torsion needs closed box sections to carry shear. Rule load cases were checked with the Hill criterion:

  • Main hoop: 30 kN in x, y and z.
  • TSAC: 19 kN in x and y, 9.5 kN in z.
  • Front impact: 20 kN (250 kg at 8 g). Side impact: 7.5 kN perimeter shear.
  • Harness and anti-submarine loads.

Where it flagged stress concentrations, at the cockpit edge and the anti-intrusion plate, the thesis recommends fillets for the next iteration.

Final laminate

31 plies, 7 shapes, 19.78 kg

The final layup across the monocoque. Manufacturability was considered when defining the ply shapes. A full manufacturing study is the next step for the team.

Result

Two honest baselines

Against the first monocoque laminate, optimisation gave −65% mass and +49% stiffness. Against the IFS-07 steel spaceframe the team actually raced, the final monocoque is −48.7% mass and +77.7% stiffness.

Tools I build to answer engineering questions.

Managing Composites · Python

Laminate search and optimisation

At Managing Composites I built a Python tool that screens thousands of candidate laminates against a baseline. It combines classical laminate theory with first-ply failure. Engineers get lighter layups that keep the bending, torsional and strength properties they need.

▮▮▮ All material data and results are blurred for confidentiality

Theory
Classical laminate theory (ABD matrices), first-ply failure with Tsai-Wu and Tsai-Hill strength ratios over 6 load cases
Solves for
Ply count, material selection, ply angles, symmetry and balance, and mixed materials between plies
Two modes
Optimisation near a baseline laminate, or search by target stiffness
Output
Ranked candidates on areal weight against D11, D22, D33 and min strength ratio, with a “best balanced” pick

MSc thesis groundwork · 2027 F1 Technical Regulations

RULEMAP: every rule, mapped onto the car

A desktop app I built to analyse Section C of the 2027 F1 Technical Regulations (Issue 2, 05/08/2026). It places every mechanical rule on a blueprint of the car, rebuilds the reference volumes from the regulation’s own coordinates and cross-checks them against the text.
